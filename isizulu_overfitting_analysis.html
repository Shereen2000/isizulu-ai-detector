Training dynamics, generalisation gap, and bias-variance diagnostics  |  26 May 2026

⚠️
Verdict: Mild / Controlled Overfitting

The model exhibits transient overfitting at epoch 3 — training loss reached near-zero while validation loss spiked to 0.187 (from a best of 0.032). Early stopping correctly identified epoch 2 as the optimal checkpoint and halted training at epoch 4 after the patience counter expired. The saved model generalises well: validation F1 0.994, Test-1 F1 0.993, Test-3 F1 0.982. The degree of overfitting is modest and was handled automatically by the training setup.

4. Overfitting Indicator Checklist

Training loss → near zero From step ~1200 (epoch 2.3), batch loss collapsed to 0.0000. The model perfectly memorises the training set — a prerequisite for overfitting.
Validation loss spike at epoch 3 Eval loss rose from 0.032 (E2) to 0.187 (E3), a 5.8× increase, while training loss was simultaneously near zero. Classic overfitting signature.
Train ROC-AUC = 1.0 from epoch 2 Perfect separation on training data from epoch 2 onward signals the model has fully memorised label boundaries. ROC-AUC on eval remains 0.9997–0.9999 — still high but below 1.
Early stopping triggered correctly The EarlyStoppingCallback fired after patience=2 epochs of no improvement. Best checkpoint at epoch 2 (F1 0.994) was preserved. The mechanism worked as intended.
Small and stable accuracy gap Except at epoch 3 (gap 0.014), train and eval accuracy stay within 0.004–0.007 of each other. The generalisation gap is narrow, indicating the model is not severely overfitting.
Test set performance holds up Test-1 (2 022 samples): accuracy 0.993, MCC 0.985. Test-3 (224 samples): accuracy 0.982, MCC 0.965. Both close to validation results — the saved model generalises to unseen data.
Gradient norm spikes Grad norm reached 308, 529, and 578 during epoch 1, indicating instability during initial fine-tuning. Norms collapsed to <1 by epoch 1.7, suggesting the model over-adapted to certain batches early on.
No false negatives on test sets FN = 0 on Test-1, Test-2, and Test-3. The model never misses a machine-written IsiZulu text. This consistent behaviour across held-out sets suggests robust feature learning, not just memorisation.

Conclusion: The model experienced a clear but transient overfitting episode at epoch 3, characterised by a near-zero training loss and a 5.8× spike in validation loss. This is a common pattern when fine-tuning large pre-trained language models on relatively small datasets — the model memorises training examples once the task-specific signal is exhausted. The training setup's early stopping (patience = 2) and checkpoint saving functioned correctly, selecting the epoch-2 checkpoint (step 1024) as the best model. The saved model's strong performance on three independent test sets (F1 ranging 0.982–0.994) confirms that the overfitting was controlled and the final model generalises well to unseen IsiZulu text.
Potential mitigations if further training is needed: (1) Reduce learning rate or add weight decay to slow memorisation. (2) Increase dropout in the classifier head. (3) Add more training data to raise the overfitting threshold. (4) Reduce training epochs to 2 and remove early stopping overhead.
